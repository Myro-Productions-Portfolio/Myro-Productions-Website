Scene 02 / Proposed

Discover what is being built

Light paths connect project destinations. Selecting one settles the camera and opens a stable reading surface with imagery, a demonstration, development notes, and relevant links.

Navigation: a direct project index always provides an alternative to spatial exploration.

Static storyboards only. Copy and scene mechanics are proposals. The existing hero is a flat image; scrolling into it requires new depth assets or scene geometry, not just enlarging the image.

Interaction study

Particle fields

Particles respond to pointer or touch through attraction, repulsion, and damping. Their movement follows the same visual currents as the landscape.

Interaction study

Scientific visualizations

Explore vector fields, wave interference, or graph propagation as bounded experiments. Give each a small set of controls and a clear visible response.

Interaction study

Digital transitions

Investigate a point cloud resolving into a project image, a wireframe becoming a surface, or signal lines assembling a diagram. Choose a consistent transition vocabulary.

Systems to design and reuse
  • Scene composition: foreground, midground, background, camera path, and stable text layers.
  • Assets: separated hero layers or terrain geometry, particle sprites, material treatments, and accurate project media.
  • Interaction: pointer/touch response, particle behavior, and predictable reset or return controls.
  • Transitions: shared timing, state changes, and continuity between the landscape and project details.
  • Showcase components: project destinations, experiment controls, readable detail panels, and work-in-progress labels.
  • Quality: adaptive visual complexity, reduced-motion scenes, keyboard access, and a direct project index.

Review checklist
  • Does the experience feel like one digital world?
  • Does interaction reveal the work and demonstrate deliberate technical craft?
  • Can visitors pause, read, navigate directly, and return to their place?
  • Are simulated effects and sample data distinguished from live systems?
  • Does the experience remain usable on touch devices, with keyboard navigation, and with reduced motion?
  • Are heavy effects limited or paused when they are not visible?
